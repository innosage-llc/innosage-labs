## Independent lifecycle design investigation

The prior actual-official-backend subgroup/TERM-resistant-grandchild counterexample and failed cleanup attempt remain retained. We did not replace them with a passing group-inheritance test.

No supported unprivileged Darwin primitive was established that atomically owns arbitrary future descendants through early-parent exit, subgroup creation and setsid:

- Apple's [event.h](https://github.com/apple-oss-distributions/xnu/blob/main/bsd/sys/event.h) marks NOTE_TRACK/NOTE_CHILD unsupported since 10.5. Fork notification is not atomic descendant registration.
- Spawn flags establish initial process state, not a restriction on descendants changing groups/sessions. Apple's [kern_prot.c](https://github.com/apple-oss-distributions/xnu/blob/main/bsd/kern/kern_prot.c) does not establish a supported sandbox grouping-denial operation at setpgid/setsid. We did not invent one.
- Polling PID/parent/session membership cannot prove race-free ownership through reparenting and PID reuse. Coalition/EndpointSecurity approaches require authority outside this task's permission-neutral scope.
- GNU [timeout --foreground](https://www.gnu.org/software/coreutils/manual/html_node/timeout-invocation.html) avoids its own separate group, but explicitly excludes children from timeout. It could support only an explicitly audited cooperative group-preserving command contract, not the required arbitrary subgroup/setsid cases. Mole has no verified documented foreground switch here. No vendor engine was patched, private override used, privileged service installed, or permission requested to simulate a repair.

The smallest separate architecture decision would be a supported upstream lifetime contract with pinned/audited command routing and explicitly constrained guarantees, or stronger platform isolation with separately approved authority. Neither is silently substituted for reliable native descendant containment. Purge remains fail-closed before external subprocess launch. Other sources gain no new containment guarantee.
